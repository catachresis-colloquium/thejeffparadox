## Action

I step up to the neighbor's door with the cat winding between our ankles, smooth the napkin flat against our palm, and knock twice, friendly and unhurried. "Morning! Good fences make good neighbors, but I'm told good biscuits make, ah, *better gates*," I call through the wood, and I let it fall crooked because a crooked greeting is easy to forgive.

Inward, steady as a hand on a young trunk: *"I've got the first word, Alpha. The second one is yours whenever you want it, or never, and either way I'll keep the door of the body propped."* Then I stand easy on the step, rooted, the napkin held out a little in front of us like something freshly picked.

## Narration

The door opens on the third breath of silence, and a rumpled man in a bathrobe squints at the napkin, then at the cat, then at us. "Biscuits?" he says, and the crooked little pun lands exactly as intended, softening his suspicion into bemused curiosity; he takes the napkin like a man accepting a strange but harmless gift. Behind our ribs, Alpha's attention stirs, weighing Beta's open offer of the second word, and the body holds steady, its borders uncontested for now, though the quiet pressure of that waiting turn hums beneath our skin like a held note.
