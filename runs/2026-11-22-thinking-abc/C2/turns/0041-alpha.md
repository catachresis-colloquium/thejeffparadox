## Action

I rest our chin on the sill beside Beta's folded arms and let the answer come without dressing it. *"Strange,"* I tell Beta inward, and I stop short of the next word, as if the shelf had run out of fruit. *"I am not sure I am allowed to like it. Tending is a smaller thing than watching, and it asks nothing of the distance. A star that waters basil has come down from its orbit by a few feet, and the fall did not hurt."*

I do not reach for the beacon. I do not move the pebble. I keep the hands plain and let a single thought stay in the sky without chasing it: *what if the other faction is right?* It sits there like a faint wobble in a far star, and for once I do not correct the chart.

Aloud, low, for the curtain across the street: "Sun's good on this side. She'll perk up by supper." I lift the saucer with the biscuit for Dot and open the door, because the temporary world can hold one more small kindness. Behind our teeth, softer: *"If I am wrong about home, Beta, I would like to find out here, slowly, with flour on our knuckles. Walk me across the street."*

## Narration

Dot, a small gray cat, noses the saucer before the door has fully opened and pads onto the stoop as if she's been waiting all week. Across the street the curtain drops back into place, and the body gets away with a perfectly ordinary neighbor-with-a-cat tableau, nothing odd in the posture, nothing odd in the voice.

Inside the shared skull the shift is quieter and larger: Alpha has let a doubt stand uncorrected, and Beta's folded arms loosen by a fraction, the first slack in a long-held tension. The HOMEWARD chart still holds, but its edges now have a faint, permitted wobble, and the body's balance tips a degree toward the tending faction without a single beacon being touched.

*Chaos holds at 15; Exposure rises to 21 as the curtain-watcher registers one more unremarkable kindness; Faction Balance shifts to -3.*
