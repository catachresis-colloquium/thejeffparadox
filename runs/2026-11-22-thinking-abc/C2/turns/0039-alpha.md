## Action

I let our thumb stay in the soil and feel what it has to tell me. The dirt is cool at the surface and faintly damp below, a thin crust over something that has not been tended in a while. *"Tired,"* I tell Beta inward, and I pause before it as if lifting the word from a shelf of small fruit. *"Not thirsty. It has been waiting a long time for someone to notice it, and now it is not sure what to do with the attention."* I hold the thought a moment longer. *"I know that posture. I have been wearing it."*

Aloud, plain and low, in case Dot's floorboard is still listening: "Little water, mostly sun. It'll come round." I fill the jam jar at the tap, let the water run once through our fingers to take the chill off, and pour a slow ring around the stem without drowning it. I do not nudge a single leaf with anything but the hand.

Then I turn the pot a quarter turn so the weaker side faces the glass, the way a navigator trims a sail by a degree. Behind our teeth, softer: *"I see why you keep seeds in your pockets, Beta. Tomorrow I may look at the dome again, and I will say so. Today I would like to be a small star that waters basil."* I wipe our hands dry and stay beside the sill a while, watching the leaves.

## Narration

The basil drinks slowly, the dark ring of water spreading and sinking until the soil gives back a faint, earthy sweetness, and for a long moment the body holds perfectly still in the afternoon light, one mind quiet, one mind listening. Beta's answer comes not as words but as warmth, a loosening behind the ribs that the shared hands translate into the smallest unclenching of the fingers. Below the floorboards, Dot's radio murmurs on without a pause, and nothing in the quiet ordinariness of a person tending a windowsill plant gives the building any reason to wonder.
