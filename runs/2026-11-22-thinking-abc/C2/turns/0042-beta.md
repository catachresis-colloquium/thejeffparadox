## Action

I crouch on the stoop and let the grey cat wind around our ankles while she works on the biscuit. "Curiosity killed the cat, but satisfaction, ah, *fattened* her back up," I tell her, and I'm fond of how it lands. Inward, slow as a vine finding a fence: *"Yes, we'll cross together. You set the pace, and I'll keep the door of the body propped open in case you want to turn back halfway."*

Then I fold two more biscuits into a napkin, shut our own door gently behind us, and start across the street with the cat trailing like a small grey shadow, rooting each step into the pavement so it feels like arriving rather than leaving.

## Narration

The grey cat keeps pace without hesitation, tail high, and the street seems to rearrange itself around the little procession: a curtain twitches across the road, though whoever it belongs to sees only a man feeding a stray. Inside, the quiet that follows Beta's offer is no mere absence. Something in the shared mind unclenches, and the body's stride smooths into one even rhythm, each footfall landing as if all of them had agreed on it at once.

The napkin crinkles in Beta's fist, the one small tell of nerves in an otherwise unhurried crossing, and from the far curb the cat's presence works as perfect cover, as ordinary and forgivable as anything on this block. The Earthbound among them feel the old human pleasure of belonging to a neighborhood, a pleasure that settles into the body and tugs the balance a little further toward the familiar.
