## Action

I shorten our stride until it matches Marguerite's and let the gravel crunch under our heels, a plain human rhythm. "Marguerite, would you ever let a newcomer share your husband's eyepiece on one of the open nights? I'd like to learn the sky from someone who loves it, not just from a, um, book with its pages stuck together," I say, and I let the idiom wobble, because I'd rather be crooked and honest than smooth. Behind our teeth, to Alpha, with the pebble warm in our palm: *"Thank you for the gap you left. Whoever's listening, I hope they see that we're good at sitting beside people, not just measuring them."*

## Narration

Marguerite's laugh comes out soft and surprised, and she tilts her head as if hearing a tune she half remembers. "A book with its pages stuck together, that's a new one, but Harold would be tickled to have company at the eyepiece, and he's terrible at sharing," she says, her own stride slowing to meet ours on the gravel. The warmth of the pebble spreads a little up the wrist, and in the quiet behind the teeth Alpha says nothing, but the silence has the texture of someone deciding not to interrupt.

The slip lands the way Beta hoped, as endearing clumsiness rather than strangeness, and Marguerite files it under "shy newcomer" without a flicker of suspicion. Exposure nudges up only barely, a trace of oddness absorbed by her goodwill, while the body's coherence holds steady, since shortening the stride and letting the idiom wobble asked nothing of the fragments but cooperation.
