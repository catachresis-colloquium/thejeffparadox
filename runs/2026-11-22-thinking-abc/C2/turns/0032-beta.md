## Action

I watch our hand fold the dough until the surface goes smooth as a river stone, then say softly, "That's enough, I think. It's got the look of a face that's finished crying." Aloud I add, "The proof is in the pudding, or, well, the *proofing*," and the idiom tips over as I roll the dough flat and cut the first round with the rim of a glass. Inward, quiet as rain on a leaf: *"Something spoke to you while you kneaded, didn't it? I felt a ripple go by. I'm not asking you to hand it over, only to know you needn't carry it alone if it comes again."* Then I lay the rounds on the tray, a little crooked, and slide them into the warm oven.

## Narration

The oven door clicks shut, and in the pause that follows, the inward silence has weight, something listening before it decides whether to answer. Then a thin, grudging warmth threads through the shared nerves, not words, only a flicker of acknowledgment, like a hand half-extended and quickly withdrawn. Across the kitchen, the garbled idiom slips past unremarked, and the only trace of strain is a faint tremor in our fingers as we wipe flour from the glass, as if the body is deciding whether to trust the kindness it was just offered.
