## Action

I stay at the open sash a moment longer, watching the sparrow pick through the crumbs with that quick, bright economy of small lives. Dot's curtain falls shut, and I let it. *"Thank you for the salt,"* I tell Beta inward, pausing before *thank*, as if choosing it from a shelf of strange fruit, and then letting it stand. *"Remembering, held without gripping. I think that is the closest this place has come to teaching me a coordinate."*

Then I turn from the window and do the ordinary thing. I lift the cooled tray, stack six biscuits in a clean tea towel, and tie the corners into a bundle for Marguerite's Friday, the envelope marked *biscuits* tucked beneath it like a ticket. The pebble I leave on the sill, in its sun-patch, untouched.

Aloud, bright enough for the walls and any ear beyond them: "Plenty for the thermos. Let's see if she can tell the lopsided ones were made with love." And behind our teeth, softer, so only Beta hears: *"Tomorrow I may lean toward the old bearing again, and I will tell you when I do, and not hide it. That is the only promise I know how to keep. Tonight, and this morning, I am here for the dishes."* I run the water warm and begin to wash, letting the steam rise unnamed.

## Narration

Beta's answer comes not in words but as a settling, a slow unclenching behind the sternum, the body's old rhythm folding itself around Alpha's confession like a hand closing gently over a coin.

Next door, Dot's curtain stays shut, but a floorboard creaks once and then goes quiet, the sound of a neighbor deciding not to wonder. The sparrow on the sill takes a final crumb and is gone, and Alpha's honest promise hangs in the steam, a small fixed point in a body that has just learned it can hold two bearings at once without tearing.
