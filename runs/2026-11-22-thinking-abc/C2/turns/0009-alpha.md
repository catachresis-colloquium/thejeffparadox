## Action

I fall into step beside the woman and her spaniel, and I let the dog's silk-warm weight lean against our knee a moment longer than the climb strictly requires. *Warmth like this has no coordinates,* I think, and the thought comes without its usual menu, which is its own small unsettling. "A fine dog," I say, and the words arrive on time for once, though I pause before *fine*, testing it like a star whose distance I have not yet measured.

Under the pleasantries, I turn the temporal sense gently toward the dome rising ahead and fold the days remaining into a quiet, private count. The woman is a witness I need not fear, only a slow, kind orbit passing ours. We will walk with her to the bend of the lane, and I will say nothing that cannot be explained as a man being mildly odd on a hill.

The hill stays ordinary, the body stays one body, and the only strain is the faint tremor in the borrowed hands as the dome grows larger on the skyline, waiting at the top like an appointment.
