## Action

I feel the tremor where two sets of fingers argue over the mug, so I loosen my half of the grip and let the warmth of the china do the holding. "Too many cooks spoil the, ah, *steeping*," I murmur to the kitchen, and I smile as it tips over, because the room is ours and a crooked sentence costs nothing here. Inward, slow as a root finding the damp seam in a stone: *"You can have the whole cup, Alpha. I'd rather share the sigh than win the hand. When Friday comes and Marguerite is across the table, I'd like us to decide together how much of this morning is ours to keep and how much is worth telling, and no one has to decide that today."* Then I take a second, smaller sip so the body has something ordinary to do with its breath, and let the sweetness settle.

## Narration

The mug tips and spills across the counter, and for a heartbeat the body holds still, caught between two nervous systems that have just stopped fighting over it. Tea runs toward the edge in a bright amber line while Alpha, thrown off balance by the sudden slack in the grip, feels the contested fingers flood with a warmth that is neither hers nor Beta's alone. The second sip goes down unremarkable and human, and the kitchen is quiet, the spill the only evidence that anything strange happened here.

Beta's concession lands as a soft, unexpected weight on the shared mind, and Alpha does not answer. The mention of Friday and Marguerite hangs in the silence like an unsigned contract, and the deferral costs Beta nothing today but binds them both to a reckoning later. A small spill and a muttered half-joke in an empty room barely register on the outside, though the body's coherence ripples once with the strain of two fragments going gentle at the same time.
