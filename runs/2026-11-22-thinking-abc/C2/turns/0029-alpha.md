## Action

I stand at the sill with the envelope under our fingertips and let the sunrise sit on the paper a moment before I answer Beta. *"Good morning,"* I say inward, pausing before *morning* as if selecting it from a menu, and finding it fits. *"Biscuits, then. A small, absurd treaty, and I will honor it."*

Aloud, I give the room a plain, unhurried line, in case the curtain across the street is still listening: "Flour, butter, and a patient oven. Easy enough." I turn from the window and walk to the kitchen at an ordinary pace, no navigator's stride, rolling the shoulders as a man does when he means nothing by it. I do not look at the dome through the glass, though I feel its bearing sitting at the back of the neck like a held coordinate.

While I take down the flour, I let the temporal sense drift loose and low, not toward the beacon, only toward Friday, to confirm the lantern I set on that far shelf is still burning. It is. *"I will bake beside you,"* I tell Beta, *"and I will ask for nothing today. But when the thermos is in Marguerite's hands, remember that you promised me a question answered together."*

## Narration

The flour bag comes down off the shelf with the unremarkable thump of a man who has done this a thousand times, and across the street the curtain stays exactly as still as before, which is its own kind of answer. Alpha's temporal reach, thin and quiet, touches Friday and comes back warm, a small success that sits easier in the body than any grand navigation would; Beta feels the steadiness of it and the hand on the sifter stops trembling. The bargain is struck without argument, a truce measured in butter, and for the first time this morning the body seems to belong to both of them at once.

Beneath it, the dome's bearing keeps its patient pull at the back of the neck, and Alpha's refusal to look at it is itself a held breath that cannot last all day. A promised question now hangs over the thermos and Marguerite's hands, a debt that neither fragment has forgotten.
