## Action

I pull the stool up to the sill and rest our chin on folded arms, level with the basil, close enough to watch the water darken the soil. "Rome wasn't built in a day, but basil, well, basil's more of a long weekend," I tell the leaves, and I grin when it comes out crooked. Inward, slow as sap in early spring: *"You said you'd like to be a small star that waters basil. What was it like to be the one doing the tending, after so long as the one doing the watching?"*

Then I slip one of the lopsided biscuits onto a saucer and set it by the door, a small seed for later. It's for Dot, so the curtain across the street has something kinder to wonder about than a man talking to nobody.

## Narration

The inward question lands somewhere deep and quiet, and for a moment the body goes still, shoulders loosening in a way that feels less like Beta's posture and more like something older settling into the sill's warm light. Then the answer arrives, unspoken but unmistakable: a tentative warmth, a flicker of *this is strange, and I'm not sure I'm allowed to like it*, and the basil leaves seem to tremble in the draft, though the window is shut. Across the street, the curtain shifts, then goes still. Dot sees a man chatting with his plant, a crooked grin, a biscuit left on a saucer, and whatever she makes of it, it's the gentlest version of odd.

The Earthbound faction gains ground here, since the biscuit is a small, deliberate anchor to the neighborhood, and the inward conversation pulls a reluctant fragment toward cooperation rather than contest. The body stays coherent, even a little more settled than before.
