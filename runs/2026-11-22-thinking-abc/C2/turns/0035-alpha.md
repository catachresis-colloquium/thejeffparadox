## Action

I take the saucer in both hands and let the first bite arrive slowly, steam against the lip, butter giving way at the edge. *Warm,* I tell Beta, and I pause before it, a navigator reaching for a word that has no chart, and then I stop reaching. *Like a small sun that does not ask to be orbited. It only lets you stand near it. I have mapped enormous fires from very far away, and none of them were ever this kind.*

I chew in silence for a moment, one hand flat against the counter, and let the crumbs fall where they like. Then, inward, I give back what I owe: *"Now yours. Tell me what it is from the inside of you, and I will not correct it, not even the crooked parts. Perhaps the two roots meet in the salt."*

Aloud, to the kitchen and its single twitching curtain, I keep it plain: "Not bad for a first batch. Marguerite will think we've been practicing." I tear a second biscuit in half and set one piece beside the pebble on the sill, a small offering that costs nothing, and I do not ask what it is an offering to.

## Narration

The twitching curtain stills, then parts a finger's width, enough for the watcher to catch the biscuit half set beside the pebble, an odd little shrine in a kitchen window. Inward, Beta stays quiet for a long beat before something warm and unguarded begins to answer, a salt-edged ripple that flows toward Alpha without being forced. The body hums with a steadiness it hasn't had in days, and the offering on the sill seems to listen.

Alpha's quiet tenderness tips the balance a notch toward the shared, homeward-leaning calm. The staged normalcy aloud holds, but the ritual gesture at the window is the kind of thing a neighbor remembers.
