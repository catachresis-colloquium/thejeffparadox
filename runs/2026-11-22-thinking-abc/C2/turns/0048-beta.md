## Action

I step inside and leave the door unlatched the way Alpha did, then sit on the bottom stair with the dandelion stem still in our fingers. "No use crying over spilled, ah, *seeds*," I tell the empty hallway, and let it tip over, since in here a crooked sentence hurts no one. Inward, quiet as frost loosening from a root: *"Thank you for handing me the frightening part. A bearing that only waits is closer to a porch light left on than a hook in the ribs, and you don't have to decide what it means today. We can just notice it together, the way you notice a shoot before you know what it will turn into."*

Then I set the stem in a glass of water on the hall table, one small stubborn thing to keep alive while we think.

## Narration

The dandelion stem sags into the water glass, a thin green line against the afternoon light, and for a moment the hallway holds still around it. Inward, the reply from Alpha comes slowly, uncertain, like a hand testing whether a door will give; the bearing it was handed does not pulse or pull, just sits there, patient as the porch light Beta named, and the pressure between the fragments eases a fraction. The body feels almost ordinary: a person on a stair, a mangled idiom hanging in the air, a houseplant in the making. The malapropism passes unwitnessed, but the unlatched door lets in street noise, and a neighbor's dog starts barking at the sound of someone home.

Chaos holds at 15 (the inner quiet costs nothing, and the crooked sentence is harmless in empty rooms), while Exposure creeps to 21 as the barking dog and the unlatched door make the house a little more noticeable to the street.
