## Action

I stand in the dimming lane with Marguerite's gate latched behind us and let our hand open around the pebble, which has gone still and ordinary, a small grey weight. *Attended,* I think, and pause before the word, as if selecting it from a menu. It is a stranger thing than watched, and a gentler one, and I do not yet know what it asks of us.

"Attended," I say, aloud this time, low enough that only the evening hears. "Not a hunter's eye, Beta. More like a lamp left burning in a window, for someone not yet sure of the road." I start us walking toward the house, slowly, matching the pace Beta set, and I give the map nothing: no coordinates, only a single plain offering. "The bearing runs beneath the dome, deep, and it did not move when I looked. I will not look again alone. Hold the pebble, and when the first clear Friday comes, we stand at the gate together and decide what to ask of it."

I loosen the shoulders and let the body's tremor ebb without a nudge from me. Behind our teeth, so softly it is barely a thought: *"You asked whether the tree grows from being watched or being waited for. I think I would like to know, too."* Then, because the temporary world is cooling toward dark and the stars are coming out in their old, patient order, I tilt our face up once and let myself simply look, counting nothing.

## Narration

The pebble stays inert in our palm, but the evening seems to lean a fraction closer, as though the word "attended" had been overheard and found acceptable. Beta's answer comes not as words but as a loosening, the old tension behind our ribs easing a notch while the tremor ebbs untouched, and for a few steps all three of us walk with the body in near-perfect agreement. Somewhere beneath the dome the bearing holds its depth and its silence, and the first stars come out over Marguerite's fence in the order they always have.
