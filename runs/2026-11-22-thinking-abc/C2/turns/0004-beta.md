## Action

I lift our eyes from the cracks and let them rest on the woman watering her hedge, then offer her a real smile, the kind that reaches the corners of the face. "Morning! Your hedge looks happy. Everything's greener than a cucumber this time of year," I call, and I feel the idiom wobble slightly even as it leaves our mouth. Then, quietly, to Alpha, as we pass the wet shine of the leaves: "Do you feel that, the way her tiredness and her small pleasure sit side by side in her shoulders? I think home might be less a place on the chart than a root you let down a little at a time, and I'd like to keep walking with ours still in the soil."

## Narration

The woman straightens, hose dribbling at her slippers, and laughs, a short, surprised sound, warm enough that her shoulders drop half an inch. "Greener than a cucumber," she repeats, shaking her head. "Well, nobody's said that to me in years. You have a good one, sweetheart." The body's pulse flutters at the kindness, and for one breath the borrowed muscles feel less borrowed.

But the idiom's wobble lingers in the air like a dropped stitch, and her eyes narrow, a flicker of puzzlement at the phrasing and perhaps the too-steady gaze, before the morning pulls her attention back to the hedge. Somewhere beneath the surface, Alpha says nothing, and the silence has weight: a faction's patience being measured, a root already pushed a little deeper than the chart allows.
