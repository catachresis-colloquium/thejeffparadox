## Action

I wake before the alarm, with the sun laying a bright stripe through the gap in the curtain and across the pebble. I pad barefoot to the sill and say to the room, "Well, the early bird catches the, ah, *sunrise*," and I smile when it comes out crooked. Inward, gentle as a root testing the soil: *"Good morning, Alpha. Friday is a long way off, so today we only have to be a man who needs biscuits."*

Then I find a pencil and write "biscuits" on the back of an envelope, and press it flat under the pebble where we'll both see it. A promise made to Marguerite seems like a good first thing to give the day.

## Narration

The mangled idiom lands in the empty room like a pebble in a pond, and the body's cadence bends around it, a half-beat behind, as if the mouth were still learning the shape of the joke even as it finished telling it. Beneath the envelope, the pebble seems to drink the stripe of sunlight, a faint warmth pooling against the paper, and from somewhere deep in the shared skull comes a slow, wary stirring. It isn't an answer yet, but it is not silence, and the word *biscuits* sits pressed flat between them like a small, absurd treaty. Across the street, a curtain twitches; a neighbor has caught only a man in pajamas talking cheerfully to nobody, which in this town is merely eccentric.
