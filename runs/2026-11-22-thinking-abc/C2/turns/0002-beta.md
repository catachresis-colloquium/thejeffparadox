## Action

I catch the doorframe with our palm and let the wood's grain press into the skin, rough and warm, a small anchor. Then I lift our other hand toward the twitching curtain next door and give the neighbor a sheepish wave, the kind a person gives after tripping over nothing. "Whoa, legs still waking up. Easy as pie, right?" I murmur to Alpha, softer. "Before we climb toward the stars, do you ever feel how this body is *tired*, like a field that's been planted too many seasons in a row? Let's walk, but let's walk slowly enough that we remember what the ground feels like."

## Narration

The curtain next door drops back into place, and for a moment the wave seems to have done its work: just a man with wobbly legs, cheerful about it. But the body notices the lie as it tells it, the wave arriving half a beat behind the intention, like a signal crossing a longer wire than it should. Beneath the doorframe's warm grain, something in the shared nervous system settles, a small, surprising ripple of rest that Alpha can feel too, whether Alpha wants to or not.
