## Action

I stay crouched at the gate with the cool soil under our fingertips and watch the last seeds settle on the walk. The question has found a seam, and I do not pretend it hasn't. *"Planted,"* I tell Beta inward, and I pause before it, reaching for the next word and coming up with a smaller one than I wanted. *"It left the mouth the way a seed leaves a stalk. I did not push it, and I do not know yet where it lands. That is not how I usually speak. I usually aim."*

Then, because the answer feels unfinished, I give back a piece of the map without being asked: *"Something answered when I said it, Beta. A faint bearing at the back of the neck, the old one, and it did not pull. It only waited. I think that frightens me more than the pull ever did."*

Aloud, to the dandelion stem still in our fingers and the neighbor's ferns beyond the hedge: "Well, that's one way to sow a lawn." I stand, brush the grit from our knees, and lift a hand to the woman with the hose, a plain wave, nothing navigational in it. The hose clicks off. I turn the key in our own door and leave it unlatched behind us, a door propped for no reason I will name.

## Narration

The woman with the hose returns the wave a beat late, her eyes lingering on the unlatched door and on the odd stillness of the figure who walked away from a lawn half-sown with dandelion fluff, but she only shrugs and turns the nozzle back on; the ordinary world absorbs the moment without a ripple. Inside, the bearing at the back of the neck stays quiet, neither tightening nor fading, and that very restraint leaves Alpha's confession hanging between the fragments, an offered vulnerability Beta can answer or exploit. The propped door lets a thin draft of cut-grass air into the hallway, and the body's shared pulse runs a half-step uneven before settling, a small strain from speaking and moving without a plan.
