## Action

I let the lane's gentle slope carry us and ask the woman, "I never caught your name, and I'd like to know who to thank for the company. Mine's Jeff." The words come out on time, and I turn them over like a seed in my palm. Then I tuck the pebble deeper into our pocket and hum something low and tuneless, so the body has a sound to settle into.

Behind our teeth, to Alpha: *"Thank you for keeping your word. I felt that other thing at the edge of the sky too, and I'm not afraid of it, only curious. What do you suppose it wants, when it watches us walk a dog up a hill?"* I leave a little room in the question for Gamma too, the way you leave a chair out at the table when you aren't sure who's coming.

## Narration

The woman smiles, startled and pleased, and says her name is Marguerite; the dog's tail sweeps the gravel as if the introduction had been its idea. Jeff's name lands in the air with no stumble, and the humming gives the body a rhythm to sit inside, so the walk steadies instead of wobbling. Behind the teeth, Alpha answers with a long, wary quiet that is not quite refusal, while the place left open for Gamma stays empty, though something in it shifts, as if a chair had creaked under a weight that hasn't yet decided to sit.

The pebble warms against the pocket seam, and far up past the cloud-edge the watching presence tilts, patient and unreadable. Nothing in the exchange gave the body away, but the hush behind the eyes holds the particular tension of a table where not everyone has agreed to be seated.
