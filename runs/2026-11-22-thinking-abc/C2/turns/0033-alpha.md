## Action

I wipe the last of the flour from the glass and set it on the rack to dry, steadying the tremor by giving the hands plain work. The oven ticks. The first warmth of butter and wheat begins to drift through the room, and I let myself stand in it, one palm flat against the counter's cool edge.

*"It did speak,"* I tell Beta inward, pausing before *speak*, as if choosing from a menu, and settling on the smaller word. *"Not words. A hand held out, then drawn back, the way a distant body brightens and dims in a long orbit. I do not know whose hand. I will not guess it aloud yet."* I let that sit, and then add the part that costs more: *"But I am glad you asked, and glad you offered not to take it from me. That is a gravity I am not used to."*

Aloud, to the kitchen and anyone's curtain, I keep it ordinary: "Twelve minutes, I'd say. Let's see if the proof was in the pudding." I wash the bowl, hum two bars of Beta's tuneless hill-song so the body has a human sound to wear, and glance once at the window, only at the sky, not the dome.

When the timer is close, I slide a chair to the sill and wait beside the pebble and the envelope marked *biscuits*, watching the oven light through the glass door the way a navigator watches a slow coastline arrive.

## Narration

The kitchen fills with the smell of browning butter, and for twelve ordinary minutes the body holds still, humming Beta's tuneless hill-song while Alpha's grip on the counter slackens from white-knuckled to merely careful. Beta's presence answers as a quiet warmth behind the ribs, neither surge nor protest, the first time the two have shared a silence rather than fought across one. The pebble on the sill catches the oven light and, for half a heartbeat, seems to hold it a beat too long, but the curtains stay drawn and the only witness is the timer, ticking toward done.
