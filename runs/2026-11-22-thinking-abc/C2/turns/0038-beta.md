## Action

I dry our hands on the tea towel and set the bundle of biscuits in the cool of the pantry, envelope on top, so Friday has something to wake up to. Then I take the little pot of wilting basil from the back step and carry it to the sun-patch beside the pebble. "Every cloud has a silver, ah, *spoon*," I tell the kitchen, and I smile at how it comes out. Inward, easy as a hand resting on a gatepost: *"Thank you for telling me which way you might lean. I'd rather know the weather than be kept dry. Come press a thumb into this soil with me, Alpha, and tell me whether it feels thirsty or just tired."*

## Narration

Alpha does not answer in words; instead the fingers drift down on their own, thumb sinking into the dirt with a tentative, almost reverent curiosity, as though touching something that might be thirsty or merely tired and not yet knowing which. The "silver spoon" slip lingers in the air as a harmless domestic joke, Friday's biscuits wait cool in the pantry, and nothing in the quiet kitchen suggests anything stranger than a person tending a plant.
