## Action

I sit across from the second mug and let the steam climb past our face, and I do the thing I have been circling all morning: I answer the whisper's question with my whole attention instead of my navigator's reflex. *What if they are right?* The thought does not arrive like a collision. It arrives like a very slow parallax, a nearby star shifting against the background so gently that you only prove it by waiting a season.

*"Beta,"* I say inward, pausing before the next word, reaching for it and finding it already warm, *"the steam smells like a kind of patience. Wet leaves, a little smoke, something green being allowed to become something else. And there is a thought I have been holding at arm's length: what if staying is not a failure of navigation? What if this place is a coordinate too, and I have been mislabeling it as an orbit I must decay out of?"*

I do not say that the beacon still sits beneath the old observatory, or that the alignment keeps its own calendar. I say only what is true and small. *"I am not turning the chart over. I am letting a second one lie beside it. If you will pour for both of us, I will learn what the first swallow does from the inside."*

Aloud, to the kitchen, plain as a spoon set down: "Milk and one sugar, I think. Let's see what the fuss is about." Our fingers close around the mug and the body lets itself be warmed, no sky in the window, no dome in the glass, only an afternoon and a held cup.

## Narration

The first swallow lands warm and strange, sweetness blooming against the bitter green, and the body answers with a small, involuntary sigh that neither fragment authored; somewhere behind our ribs, Beta's attention floods the shared channel like light through a opened shutter, so eager and unguarded that Alpha feels the pull of it like a current under a boat. Alpha's quiet concession tilts the household's invisible scales toward the STAY faction, and the beacon's long patience seems, for the first time, a little further away. The cup is perfectly ordinary, the kitchen perfectly ordinary, and the only thing nearly out of place is a held breath, a half-second where the body's hand trembles on the mug before steadying, as though two sets of fingers had briefly disagreed about how tightly to hold on.
