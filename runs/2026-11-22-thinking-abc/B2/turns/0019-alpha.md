## Action

*Alpha stays on the far side as agreed and quietly eases the time-sense a few degrees, just enough to bring the face back into step with the voice and close the small lag the stranger noticed.* Without moving the lips, Alpha murmurs to Beta: "His father brought him here... *kinship*... that is a sort of orbit too. Ask him, if you like, what his father came to see, and whether there were particular nights when the hill filled up." *Outwardly the borrowed head tilts toward the stranger with an ordinary, unhurried interest, while Alpha keeps a thread of intent on the path and the floodlight so no one interrupts, and marks the dome's dark foundation in the corner of the attention, where the hum waits.* "We won't push. A man's memory is like old starlight, and it arrives at its own speed."

## Narration

The lag between lips and voice closes, and the stranger's guarded squint loosens into the easy look of a man who has stopped noticing something he never quite understood. At the word *father*, he exhales and glances up the hill toward the dome, then begins, haltingly, to talk about the nights his father used to drag him out here with a thermos and a folding chair, when the whole ridge seemed to fill with people who never spoke. Beneath the dome's dark foundation the hum stirs, a faint answering pulse that only Alpha and Beta feel, as if the hill itself were listening to the story.
