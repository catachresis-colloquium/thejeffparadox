## Action

*Alpha lets the body sit as Beta arranged it, loose and unremarkable, but behind the borrowed eyes the time-sense ripples outward and catches something that does not move the way the evening moves: a stillness at the edge of the treeline, held a beat too long.* "Wanted, or owed... *uncertain*... I think it is neither, Beta. It is closer to gravity, which does not ask whether the falling thing consents." *The face tips toward the stars as if in idle thought, while Alpha keeps the rest of the attention on that patient stillness, and says, barely above the crickets:* "Keep your warmth where it is, and keep the hands easy. Something out there is watching us the way one star watches another, and we should not let it see which of us is afraid."

## Narration

The crickets stall for half a breath, and at the treeline the stillness answers: a slow, deliberate shift of weight, as if whatever watches has just realized it is being watched in turn. Alpha's stretched time-sense bleeds into the body for an instant, so the next blink comes a fraction too slowly and the pulse in the borrowed throat beats out of rhythm with the evening. Beta's warmth holds and the hands stay easy, but the gravity Alpha named is now pulling at every fragment, and the body's calm feels rehearsed.
