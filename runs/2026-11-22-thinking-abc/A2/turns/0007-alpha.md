## Action

*I stop at the bend where the trees thin, thermos forgotten against my ribs, and look up at the steady light Beta called a porch light. The honest answer is already forming, and I find I do not reach for the armor.* "It is not like looking down, Beta. It is like being a word in a language that no longer has speakers. We were *meant* to be small in that light, and no one asked whether the small thing wished to be anywhere else." *I pause, selecting.* "...Lonely. That is the closest of your words. I had not known it applied to me until this body made me say it aloud."

*I crouch and press two fingers to the frost-stiff fern Beta touched, feeling it crumple and spring back, a small motion I watch as one watches a distant instrument. Without deciding to, I ease the cold off Beta's knuckles where the thermos has leached the warmth, a minor thing, a nudge of the odds toward comfort.* "Your fern returns without knowing it left. I think I envy that. Walk on. The summit is clear tonight, and I would rather you hear the rest of the names where the sky is widest."

*Behind the even voice, the beacon sits beneath the old observatory like a held note, and for the first time the pull toward it feels less like purpose than like a question I am afraid to hear answered.*

## Narration

The fern's frost-stiff fronds spring back a half-second too smoothly, and Beta's numbed knuckles flush warm in a way no thermos could manage, a small wrongness that Beta registers only as relief and files away to puzzle over later. Alpha's admission lands in the shared body like a stone dropped in still water: the pull toward the beacon wavers, and for the first time the HOMEWARD resolve carries a crack that Beta's quieter, rooted instincts rush to fill with something like tenderness. The summit is still a quarter mile off, and as they climb, the steady light overhead seems to pulse once in what could be coincidence or could be an answer.
