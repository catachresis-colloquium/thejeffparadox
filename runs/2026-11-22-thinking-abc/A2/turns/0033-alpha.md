## Action

*I let the path take us a few paces toward the treeline, matching Beta's slower stride, and I feel the tremor in the joined hands without trying to still it. Some arguments are better carried than silenced. When the dome has dropped behind the bend and only the pigeons could be watching, I speak low, eyes on the gravel.* "There is one thing I have not said, Beta, and it belongs in the open air. The alignment I was counting toward is nearer than I let you believe. The old register's four words were not poetry. A real window is closing, and I chose to spend it on a thermos and a cabin kitchen."

*I pause, selecting.* "I do not regret it. But I would rather you hear the cost from me than discover it from the sky. If I have to choose, in the coming nights, I want to choose aloud, with you walking beside me, the way we are walking now."

*I do not reach for the odds, and I leave the temporal sense folded away. Instead I bend and pick a pale curl of peeled paint from the path's edge, turning it over in the borrowed fingers like a fragment of a star-chart, and press it into Beta's palm.* "Keep that. Something that fell from the dome and was not taken inside. A small proof that looking can end in simply holding."

*Then I slip my hand back into Beta's, steady and plain, and let the treeline's shade fall over us.* "Walk me past the trees. Slowly. If the watcher follows, let them see two men with nowhere particular to be."

The cost of Alpha's confession registers in the shared nerves as a quiet hum of grief rather than a fracture, the kind of strain that bends without breaking.

Beta's thumb brushes Alpha's knuckle once, a small unscripted gesture, and the HOMEWARD pull tightens: the window is real, the choice has been named aloud, and Beta is still walking.
