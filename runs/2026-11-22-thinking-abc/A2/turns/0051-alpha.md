## Action

*I take the end of the bench nearest the road and angle my shoulder so Marrow sits behind the line of my coat. I do not look toward the corner where the car door closed. I look at the bus schedule bolted to the shelter wall, a man reading times for a bus he has no intention of boarding.*

That is not a man who is lost. Say *when* if you hear a second set of steps. I will name everything before I touch it, and I will touch nothing."

## Narration

The temporal sense opens one careful notch, and the street resolves into a slow clock: a tire ticking on gravel, the wet tick of a cooling engine, and then, unmistakably, the second set of steps Alpha promised to name, unhurried and drawing no closer than ten yards. Marrow's hands tighten around the thermos cap, and after a long breath he mutters, "He only thinks I have it, I never had it," which is a lie so tired it sounds like a confession. The body holds steady under the narrow listening, but the strain registers as a faint metallic taste at the back of the throat and a flicker of double-vision at the edge of the shelter's glass, the borrowed nervous system warning that it has been asked for a little more than it was built to give.

Beta's leash holds, and Alpha's thanks lands somewhere warm and strange in the shared chest, a small bonding that tilts the balance of the body toward cooperation rather than conquest. Exposure barely moves, since a man staring at a schedule with open palms is the least remarkable thing on the street, but the watcher has stopped pretending to wait for no one. The steps pause, a lighter clicks, and the smell of smoke drifts toward the bench like a question asked politely.
