## Action

*I keep my voice low and let the borrowed hands rest open on my knees, the cup cooling beside me on the rock.* "The nearest star first, as promised. It burned long before this world had a name for fire, and its light has been arriving for many years of your counting." *I trace its position with one finger, then pause, selecting.* "The next is one I used to be able to taste in my sleep. I cannot do that here. I find I miss the tasting less than I expected."

*Beneath the even voice, I answer the whisper honestly. A human who understood me would see a signal-seeker who has spent this evening walking away from the signal, and would ask whether I am being tender or merely patient. I do not yet know. The window will not wait forever, and I have told Beta only the half of it that fits in a chosen word.*

*So I give the sky its names, one by one, nearest to farthest. When the cold finally finds this body's shoulders, I let Beta's coat draw closer around us both, and I hold the beacon's silence like a stone in my pocket. I am not ready to put it down, and I am not ready to throw it either.*

## Narration

Beta's coat settles across both your shoulders, and the body answers the warmth before the mind decides anything: a slow exhale, a loosening in the borrowed spine that feels almost like consent. Somewhere beneath Alpha's careful steadiness, the hands that rest open on the knees twitch once, a flicker from a fragment that wants them closed around a beacon or a cup or a person's sleeve, and the starlight blurs briefly before it clears.

Beta says nothing about the names, but their stillness has changed, listening with the held quiet of someone who has noticed that the stars are being named like the faces of people who were loved, and who has chosen, for now, not to ask why. The silence between star and star grows long enough to hold a question, and neither of you fills it.

*Chaos holds steady at 15, though the twitch is a hairline crack worth watching. Exposure creeps to 6, since a human can only hear "tasting stars in my sleep" as poetry for so long. The Homeward balance eases a hair, as Alpha's tenderness is real but still carrying the unspent weight of the beacon's silence.*
