## Action

*I hear the lighter click and I don't turn around. I slide the thermos cap out of Marrow's tight fingers and replace it with my warm hand, then say, for the street's benefit and ours, in an easy voice,* "Ten past the hour and the 9 is late as usual. Nobody warns you that waiting is the real commute."

*Under my breath, lips barely moving, eyes on the schedule:* "Alpha, I heard the lighter. Steps stopped ten yards back, a man who wants us to know he's there. That's not a hunter, that's a message. I'm saying *when* is not yet, but it's close. Keep your palms open and keep the metal taste to yourself. If the double-vision grows, tell me and I'll take the weight for a minute."

*Then, softly, to Marrow, my hand firm over hers:* "You told us a tired lie, and I'm not going to pry the real one out of you on a bench. But Dray's friend with the cigarette just changed what we owe each other. If he walks up, you introduce us as the people who fed you breakfast, and I do the talking. Can you give me that much?"

*I fold the empathic sense away and keep shape-shifting buried. If this man wants a conversation, he gets a tired stranger with a crooked smile, and I stand slowly, hands visible, and take one step toward the smoke, placing myself between the bench and the voice.*

"Morning," *I call, mild as milk.* "If you're waiting on the 9 too, there's room on the bench. Fair warning, it's never on time."

## Narration

The man in the smoke doesn't answer at once; he lets the cigarette burn down a quarter inch, studying Beta's open hands and mild smile the way a person studies a door he's been told is locked. Then he steps into the light, a gray-faced stranger in a courier's jacket, and says, "Dray said you'd be the polite ones." Behind Beta, Marrow's breath catches, and in the hollow of the shared body Alpha's metal taste surges, the double vision swimming for one dizzy second before Beta's steadying weight settles over it like a hand on a trembling table.

The performance holds, with no flicker of the unnatural to snag the man's eye, but "polite ones" means Dray talked, and talked to the wrong people. Exposure edges up as the street's quiet becomes a quiet someone is listening to, while Chaos stays pinned high, only barely held by Beta's grip on the body's coherence.

Beta's choice to shield Marrow and take the first words tilts the balance toward EARTHBOUND, with calm, careful humanity serving as cover for everything crowded beneath it.
