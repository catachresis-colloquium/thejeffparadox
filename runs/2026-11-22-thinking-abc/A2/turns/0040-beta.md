## Action

*I answer the stranger before the silence can harden, light and a little rueful, the way a friend covers for a friend.* "He's always been strange about the trees, I'm afraid. We came down that path ourselves, so the guess wasn't much of a guess." *I slide the sugar toward them, an ordinary courtesy, and tilt my head.* "You've got the look of someone who walked here to say something and lost the first sentence on the way. Take your time. The coffee's bad, but it's refilled for free."

*Under the table I press my knee to Alpha's, steady and plain, and let a thread of rooted warmth settle through the shared pulse to quiet the tremor in his open palm. I keep my own hands visible and relaxed, shape-shifting folded away, no reaching into anyone's memory. I only listen with the small empathic sense, toward the stranger's edges: not for secrets, just for whether the weight they carry is fear or hope. It feels like the cold lake again, someone half in, half out.*

"I'm Beta, by the way, since my friend skips introductions when he's tired." *I give it as a nickname, offhand, with a small crooked smile.* "What should we call you? And if you'd rather start with the eggs and work up to whatever the walk was about, that's allowed too."

## Narration

The stranger exhales like someone setting down a bag they've carried too far, and the stiffness leaves their shoulders. "Marrow," they say, the name arriving as a half-offer, and Beta's small empathic sense catches the verdict: mostly hope, wearing fear like a borrowed coat. Under the table, Alpha's tremor settles against Beta's knee, but his open palm still hums with a faint wrongness, and a sugar packet beside it creeps a quarter-inch across the Formica before anyone's eyes land there.

Beta's cover is smooth enough that the stranger doesn't notice, but the diner is small, and the waitress pauses mid-pour, her gaze lingering on the sliding packet. Exposure ticks up by 2. Chaos eases by 3 as the shared pulse steadies, a quiet point for the Earthbound approach.

Marrow wraps both hands around the bad coffee and draws a breath. "I'll start with the walk," they say. "I think you already know why I came."
