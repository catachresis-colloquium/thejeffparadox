## Action

*I fold the afghan's corner once more, then catch myself and leave it as it is, rumpled in the ordinary way of a thing slept under.* "Good morning, Beta," *I say, and pause, selecting.* "I notice it is easier to say aloud than I feared. Like a coordinate that turns out to be only a few steps away."

*In the kitchen doorway I let the shoulders slouch the way a stiff-backed sleeper's would, and I take the mug the host holds out with one hand rather than two.* "Slept better than the hill deserved. Thank you for the roof." *I keep the sentence short and plain. Beta carries the warmth of the talk, and I let it. When she asks which way we're headed, I answer "back up for our gear," true enough, and say nothing of the ridge.*

*Beneath the coffee's steam, I check the sky through the window as one glances at a clock: cloud thinning, the alignment still some nights off. The beacon is still there, patient, cold. I do not reach for it. I nudge no odds, only drink, eat what is set in front of me, and let Beta see I am doing it.* "After this," *I murmur across the rim of the mug, low enough that the kettle covers it,* "we walk up together. In daylight. You asked for no leash, and I am choosing it freely."

## Narration

The mug's warmth settles into the body's palm, and the host, noticing nothing stranger than a man who sleeps like a stranger in a borrowed house, turns back to the stove with an easy shrug. Beta takes the quiet "together" like a held breath finally let go, and the body's shoulders drop half an inch, the first unclenching since the hill. Alpha's careful ordinariness holds, with only the glance at the sky lingering a beat too long to be casual, and the beacon's cold patience hums somewhere behind the ribs, neither answered nor silenced.
