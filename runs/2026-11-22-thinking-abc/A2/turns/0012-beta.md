## Action

*I keep the coat drawn around us both and don't look at Alpha. Some questions land softer when the eyes stay on the sky.* "The way you say those names, nearest to farthest, it sounds like you're calling roll for people. I won't ask who's missing. I just want you to know the roll can keep going out loud, as long as you like."

*I reach over and close one of Alpha's twitching hands inside the borrowed palm, the way you'd steady a seedling in a pot you haven't chosen yet. Under the coat, I let the rooted warmth hum quietly through the shared pulse, an offer, with no pull in it.* "I'll tell you what I've noticed about leaving a porch light on: you don't do it because you're certain anyone's coming. You do it so the dark has somewhere to end. Whatever you decide about that ridge, you won't be deciding it in the dark."

*Then I tip my head back against the cold stone and let a little humor leaven the quiet.* "Go on, then, farthest star. And if this body starts shivering, I'm calling it a vote for the thermos."

## Narration

Alpha's trembling fingers go still inside Beta's grip, and for a long breath the only sound is the wind combing the dry grass below the ridge. Then the roll resumes in a low, unsteady murmur, "Sirius. Vega. Deneb..." each name a little steadier than the last, as though the warmth humming through the shared pulse gave the words somewhere to stand. When a shiver finally ripples through the borrowed shoulders, it lands as plain human cold, and the thermos is already sweating in the grass beside them.

The Earthbound current runs a shade stronger tonight, the body leaning toward the kindness that asks for nothing. Chaos eases, since the coherence of the three-way pulse has quieted the flutter in Alpha's hands. Exposure holds steady, because an alien soul calling out stars beside a friend on a ridge is, to any passing eyes, just a man who talks to the sky.
