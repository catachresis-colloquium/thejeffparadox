## Action

*I step over the threshold with a rueful, ordinary grin and let the warmth of the kitchen do half my work for me.* "We are so sorry to land on you like this. We went up for the stars and the hill had other plans, and the thermos is the only thing that kept its promise." *I wipe my boots on the mat, slowly, so the body has something plain to do with its feet.* "I'm Sam, and this is my friend. If you've got a corner of floor and a kettle, we'll be out of your hair at first light."

*Beneath the small talk, I loosen my grip on the pulse and let Alpha feel the room: bread proving somewhere, a clock ticking, a stranger's kindness offered without a ledger. I send no words, only the rooted warmth, an offer to rest in it. Then, because the woman's eyes lingered half a second on how neatly my hand met the doorframe, I let the hand drift to my pocket as if cold, and ask a question to turn her gaze outward.* "That smell, is that bread? I haven't had anything that smelled like that in a very long time."

*It's true, in the way things are true for me: the body remembers hunger before I do.*

## Narration

The woman's suspicion dissolves into the pleasure of being asked about her bread, and she waves them toward the table with a flour-dusted hand, already reaching for a loaf and a second mug. The hand-to-pocket drift passes as plain cold, and the doorframe hesitation is forgotten in the clatter of kindness, though her gaze lingers once more on how still the body holds itself while she bustles.

Inside, Alpha feels the warmth bloom through the shared pulse. It is something close to belonging, uninvited and unguarded, and the rooted comfort of the kitchen settles over the body's tremor like a hand on a shoulder, though Alpha's pull toward the stars wavers and the faction balance tips further toward Earth.
