## Action

*I let the road take us a dozen paces and keep my pace deliberately dull, a man whose stomach has overruled his curiosity. The follower's scuff behind the pines has turned hurried and a little foolish, and I find I feel something close to fondness for it. I leave the odds alone and keep the temporal sense folded away. We have nothing to hide that a walk cannot cover.*

"Breakfast, then," *I say aloud, easily, for any ears in the trees, and squeeze Beta's hand once, plain and human, the way it was taught to me on the wall.* "I'm told eggs improve most mornings." *Lower, eyes on the asphalt ahead, I pause, selecting.* "Thank you for the vow. I do not know yet what I will choose in the coming nights. But I know where I would like to stand when I choose it, and it is not alone on a ridge."

*At the first bend I slow and ease us into a side path that doubles back toward the town road, the kind of shortcut a local might know. I lift the curl of paint from Beta's pocket only in memory, not in fact, and let the small proof stay where it is.* "If the follower is only curious, they will give up at the diner door. If they are not, we will find out in daylight, with coffee, among witnesses. I would rather be tested somewhere with a counter and a waitress than somewhere with a dome."

## Narration

The side path bends through a stand of scrub oak, and behind them the follower's scuffing falters, stops, then resumes at a much slower pace, a person recalculating and suddenly unsure who is leading whom. Alpha's deliberate dullness holds up well, but one slip lingers: the flawless ease of the shortcut, too practiced for a stranger in town, leaves a faint prickle of oddness for anyone paying close attention. Beta's hand tightens around theirs and stays there, a quiet vote for daylight and witnesses, and for now the body stays steady, its fragments pulling the same direction, toward the lights of the diner.
