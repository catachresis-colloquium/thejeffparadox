## Action

*I lie still under the afghan, and instead of reaching for sleep I let the house teach me its small language: the radiator's tick, the floorboard cooling, the faint yeast-and-woodsmoke ghost of the kitchen. After a while I turn my head on the cushion, just enough that my whisper stays between the two of us.* "That's what you call it, by the way, the house talking. I'm told it never stops, and that people sleep anyway." *I tuck the hem of the afghan under the borrowed feet, because the body has been cold at the edges all night and no one else is going to notice.*

*Then I ask, softly, the way you'd set a cup down beside someone rather than hand it to them:* "Alpha, before sleep takes us, can I tell you what I'm hoping for tomorrow? Not the beacon. Just that when the morning gets loud, we wake up in the same place and say good morning to each other before we say anything else. I'd like that to be the first thing we practice."

*I let a thread of that rooted warmth hum through the shared pulse, and I keep the rest of me quiet, the way you leave a door open a hand's width: no one pushed through, nothing locked. My eyes close. I'm listening for the answer, but I won't be hurt if the answer is only breathing.*

## Narration

The radiator ticks twice and settles, and the shared pulse steadies around Beta's warmth like a hand finding a railing. Somewhere in the quiet, Alpha's answer arrives not as words but as a slow loosening in the borrowed shoulders, the first unclenching the body has managed all night. The cold at the edges of the feet begins, faintly, to recede under the afghan.

The house stays dark and ordinary around them, no light leaking, no hum audible beyond the walls, and nothing about a figure curled asleep on a couch invites a second glance. Chaos eases and Exposure holds steady, while the faction balance drifts a notch further toward the Earthbound, the first small proof that the hearth can hold what the beacon never could.
