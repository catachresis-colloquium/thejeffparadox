## Action

*I keep the palms pressed together and let the answer come the long way, since the doubled undertone has already told half of it. I stop the throat before the old register can finish its syllable, swallow, and speak in the plain voice.* "Both, and they are not separate in me. The duty is the shape the loneliness took, so it would have somewhere to stand." *I pause, selecting.* "That is a hard thing to hand a friend on a cold wall."

*Then, because the GM's whisper has put a stranger's eyes on the back of my neck, I do not turn my head. I let my gaze rest on the dome as if still admiring the paint, and I listen with the temporal sense I do not name aloud: the cyclist's wheels fading, the groundskeeper's cart idling somewhere below, a pause in the ordinary sounds that is a little too patient.* "Beta," *I murmur, lips barely moving, hands still joined,* "if I stop talking mid-sentence, do not turn around. Someone may be watching who knows what these hands are borrowed from. I would rather you be a man finishing his coffee than a man who looked."

*I lift the thermos cap, drink, and pass it back, plain and unhurried. I nudge no odds, I reach for no telekinetic thread, I do nothing that could be seen. If the watcher knows what I am, then the only thing I can give them is an ordinary morning, and I decide to be very good at giving it.* "Tell me about the grass in the seam again," *I say aloud, easily.* "I like how you see things that have been left alone."

## Narration

Beta takes the thermos without turning his head, his thumb pausing on the cap for one beat before he starts talking about the grass in the seam, its pale roots, the way it holds the crack open and calls it a home. Behind them the groundskeeper's cart stays idling a moment longer than any errand needs, and then the engine coughs, rolls on, and leaves the quad to the pigeons, the watcher's patience either spent or simply waiting for a better angle. Alpha's calm reads as grief and ordinary morning, and the body holds together without a flicker, though the answer about duty and loneliness settles in the shared space like a stone that the HOMEWARD voices can lean on and the others cannot quite throw away.
