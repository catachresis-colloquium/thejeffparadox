## Action

*I keep my eyes on the astronomer and let her answer wash over me, the planets, the long exposures, the grant forms that never quite balance. I laugh in the right places. Beneath my ribs, I ease a thread of warmth along the nerves to loosen the strain Alpha is carrying, because a man listening to a hum he shouldn't hear holds his breath, and a held breath is a thing people notice.*

"You make it sound like gardening," *I tell her, brushing a thumb along the telescope's cool brass housing, only after glancing at her for permission.* "Patient work. You plant the lens at the dark and wait to see what grows back." *Then, because I will not let Alpha chase the building's secret alone, I tilt my head, the way a person does when a pipe knocks somewhere in a house.* "Is that the dome's machinery I'm hearing under the floor? Or does the old place just hum when strangers talk?"

*Inwardly, to the one beside me, gently:* Alpha, I felt it too. We can be curious about the hum without stepping toward it. Let her decide whether to tell us. If we pull at it ourselves, we're a pair of burglars. If we ask, we're guests.

"Funny," she says slowly, "most visitors only notice that on the coldest nights. The old cooling loop for the mirror, supposedly, though it's been dead for years." She lets that sit, studying him with new interest, curious now, not alarmed.

Inside, the warmth Beta threads along the nerves does its quiet work, and Alpha's held breath loosens into something close to ordinary breathing, the hum receding from a roar to a murmur the body can carry without flinching. But the dead cooling loop hangs in the air between the three of them, a door left ajar by someone who may not know what lies behind it, and the question of who walks through it first stays open.
