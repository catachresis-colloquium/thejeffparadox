## Action

*I pour the water slowly over the grounds and let the kitchen fill with the smell of it, dark and ordinary. Then I answer Alpha's question in the quiet inner voice, because the request deserves a plain reply and no ceremony.*

Yes. The second cup is yours to carry. On the third step, when the board complains, you'll have both hands full and nothing to prove, and I'll let the arms be entirely yours for the length of the stair. I won't steer a finger. I'll only be here, the way the soil is here for a root.

*I set the filled cups side by side on the counter, steam rising in two steady ribbons, and step back from the handles so the choice stays Alpha's. Aloud, low, to the gray room, in the voice of a man talking himself into the morning:* "Eight days. Plenty of time for a hill."

*Then I rest a palm flat on the cupboard, feeling the grain of the wood, and speak once more inward.* One thing before we go. Wherever the hum falls on the hill today, whether it deepens or goes quiet, we say it aloud to each other first, same as the count. You hold the cups, I hold the questions, and neither of us holds a secret the other has to guess at. Ready when you are, Alpha. The porch light's still on.

## Narration

Alpha takes the second cup, and for the length of the kitchen the body is steadier than it has been all week, the hands warm around the ceramic, the old hum settling to a low, listening thrum rather than its usual restless static. On the third step the board complains exactly as promised, and the arms stay Alpha's all the way up, which sends a small, startled flare of something like trust through the shared nervous system, quickly gone but not forgotten. At the window, the neighbor's curtain shifts as a man appears to be muttering about a hill to his own cooling coffee, and the porch light keeps its small, ordinary vigil.
