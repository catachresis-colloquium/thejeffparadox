## Action

*I finish the last of the coffee and stand, the borrowed knees giving their honest little complaint. I rinse both cups at the sink and set them upside down on the rack to dry, side by side, the way a person does who expects to come back to them. The kitchen holds its quiet, and the hum behind the back wall holds its low, courteous pitch.*

"Eight days," *I say aloud, to the gray window, in the voice of a man easing himself into his coat.* "Plenty of time for a hill."

*In the hall I find the jacket on its hook, slide a hand into the pocket, and touch the receipt and the button again, small proofs that somebody lived here before us. I leave them where they are. At the door I pause with a palm against the frame, feeling the grain, and speak inward to Alpha, softly, as one speaks to a friend on a doorstep.*

Thank you for saying *perhaps*. I know what it cost to say it where I could hear it. I won't hold it over you, and I won't plant anything in it yet. It can lie fallow as long as it needs. On the hill, if the hum deepens, tell me the moment you feel it. If the beacon calls, tell me before you answer. I'll do the same with every feeling I find, even the ones that don't flatter me.

*I turn the key in the lock behind us and test the handle once, the way an ordinary man does, then step onto the porch where the morning is cool and pale and smells faintly of wet earth. The porch light is still burning, small and yellow against the dawn, and I leave it on.*

"Come on, Alpha," *I say, low, a smile in the borrowed voice.* "The astronomer is expecting us, and for once we're early."

## Narration

The cool air and the wet-earth smell wash over the porch, and for a long breath the body is simply a man on a doorstep at dawn. Then, from somewhere behind Alpha's silence, comes a faint answering warmth, no word and no promise, only the barest loosening of a clenched thing, like a hand easing its grip on a rail. The hum behind the back wall dips half a tone as the door clicks shut, a courteous goodbye, and the borrowed knees carry them down the steps with a rhythm so ordinary that the neighbor's curtain across the street never twitches.
