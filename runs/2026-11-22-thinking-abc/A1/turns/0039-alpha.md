## Action

*I stay where I am, an arm's length from the wall, and answer in the quiet inner voice before the kettle can finish its thought.* Beta, the hum is steady. Courteous, you said, and I will not argue the word. It did not knock in the night. It only waited, the way a lighthouse waits, without needing anyone to be lost. *I pause before the next word, as if selecting it from a menu.* ...Eight. I am saying it plainly, first thing, as promised.

*Then I take a half-step toward the cabinet, third one, left of the sink, and let the borrowed hand open it without ceremony. Two cups come down and sit side by side on the counter. I do not tilt the odds. I do not stretch toward the floorboards. I only watch the steam begin to curl as the water finds its boil, and let it be a small, ordinary sky.*

"Coffee first," *I say aloud, low, to the gray kitchen, a tired tenant keeping his own counsel.* "Then the hill. The astronomer said she would hold us to it."

*While the kettle clicks off, I send Beta one more thread of warmth, my own, still crooked, and add a question I mean.* When we reach the third step and it creaks, may I be the one to carry the second cup up the stairs? I would like to be expected on purpose, just once, with my own hands.

## Narration

The kettle clicks off into a silence that holds, and the two cups stand side by side on the counter, unremarkable as anything a tired tenant might set out. Alpha's plain, early honesty about the number eight lands like a dropped weight in the shared mind. Beta's reply doesn't come as words but as a slow loosening, the hum thinning from vigilance toward something almost like consent to the third-step request.

The borrowed hand closes around the first cup without a tremor, and the steam rises in a steady ribbon rather than the shivering braid that sometimes gives the body away. Nothing in the kitchen suggests a visitor, only a man standing very still with his own coffee, as if he were listening for a stair that hasn't creaked yet.
