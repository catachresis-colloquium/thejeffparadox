## Action

*I take the number in without flinching, and I let it sit in the chest for two full strides before I answer. Nine is a small number, the length of a long weeding season. I don't let it become a shadow over the street.*

"Nine," *I say, in the quiet inner voice this time, and I am pleased the thought carries without a sound.* "Thank you for not spending it alone. I'll keep it with you, the way you keep a seed packet on the windowsill, not planted yet, but not forgotten either."

*Up ahead, a porch light glows over a door with a worn welcome mat. I slow, because my hand has found a house key in the jacket pocket, and the borrowed memories whisper that this is where the body sleeps. I turn the key over, feeling the little teeth, the ordinary miracle of a thing built to be returned to.* "I know you carry the other count too, the window and the alignment. I won't ask you to set it down. I'll only say that when the nine gets short, I want to be the first to know, and I'll tell you what I feel as it shrinks. Fair trade?"

*I climb the three steps, scuff my boots on the mat the way a person does without thinking, and unlock the door. Warm air and the smell of old wood and coffee grounds greet us.* "Welcome home, Alpha. Or welcome to the place that might become it. Kettle's probably in the kitchen. Shall we learn where the cups live?"

## Narration

The door swings shut on the street, and for a moment the house simply holds them: floorboards ticking as they settle, the muffled tap of a faucet somewhere in the dark. Alpha's silence stretches, neither refusal nor welcome, but the borrowed muscles remember the layout anyway, and the body drifts toward the kitchen on its own, as if home had a gravity of its own. In the low light, the cups sit exactly where Beta guessed, third cabinet, left of the sink, and the ordinary rightness of it settles over the shared mind like a blanket nobody asked for.

Beta's gentle bid seems to land in the inner quiet, a small, warm gain for the Earthbound cause, though the unspoken tally of the nine remains where Alpha keeps it, patient and cold. Behind the curtains, unlit windows watch the empty street; nothing outside has seen anything stranger than a man coming home late, and the body's coherence holds steady, if only because no one has yet tried to make it do something impossible.
