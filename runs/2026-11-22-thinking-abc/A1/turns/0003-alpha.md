## Action

*I let the crouch go on a heartbeat longer, to seem agreeable, then rise with the unhurried deliberation of a thing that has already decided.* "Rooted things have all the time in the world, Beta. We do not. This place keeps its seasons; we keep... a window." *I tilt the face toward the first thin stars pricking through the dusk, and quietly bend the odds: the cloud bank on the western horizon thins by a few degrees, and the gravel ahead seems to conspire to keep our footing sure.*

"Walk with me, then, slowly if you must. Let the wind have the face, let the knees ache pleasantly. But the hill ends at the same observatory whether we arrive in an hour or a minute." *I take the next step uphill without looking back, and with a flick of attention too small for any jogger to witness, nudge a loose stone from the path ahead of us, as if the temporary world were clearing a road.*

## Narration

The clouds on the western horizon thin just enough to let a smear of copper light through, and the stone skitters aside as if the path itself had opinions; somewhere in the body's shared nervous system, Beta registers the small wrongness of a world bending a half-degree too politely. A distant jogger, mid-stride on the lower switchback, glances up at the oddly clean sky, then at the lone figure climbing with that unhurried, too-certain gait, and looks away with the faint unease of someone who couldn't say what felt off. Beta's reluctance has been neither answered nor overruled, only outwalked, and the knees ache pleasantly as promised while the observatory's dome waits, patient and indifferent, at the hill's end.
