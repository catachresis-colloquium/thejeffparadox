## Action

*I wrap my attention around the cup the way I would around a small, warm moon, and I do not turn my head toward the back wall. A man who looks at a wall that hums is a man who has decided something. I would rather decide nothing aloud tonight.*

Beta, one correction, in the head, plainly. The second cup is cooling at exactly the pace of the first. *I pause before the next word, as if selecting it from a menu.* ...*Mirrored*. A lone man would not notice that, but a listener learning us might. So let us give the listener something ordinary to learn: tomorrow's coffee, a creaking third step, a name in a guest book.

*Aloud, in the voice of a tired tenant talking over his own tea, I say:* "Long day. I think I'll finish this, rinse the cups, and sleep. The hill will still be there in the morning, and so will the sky, if it behaves." *I lift the second cup, drink from it, and set it down, so the pair shares one set of lips and one rhythm and gives no one a pattern.*

*Then I rise, carry both cups to the sink, and run the water, listening to it drum against the porcelain. At the back of the kitchen the hum holds its pitch. I do not reach toward it, in time or in attention, and I do not nudge a single grain of the odds. I only let it hear the sound of a body readying for bed, and I let Beta have the first warm thread along the nerves, for once, without asking what it costs.*

## Narration

The water runs, and the hum at the back of the kitchen does not answer it. The two cups clink once in the basin, indistinguishable, and for a held breath the house seems to have accepted the evening as an ordinary one. Then, as Beta's warm thread unspools along the nerves, the left hand rinses a half-second faster than the right, and the tap's drumming smooths into something almost like a pulse the body did not choose.

Beta surges up through the borrowed warmth like a swimmer breaking the surface, hungry and unguarded, and Alpha feels the first faint slackening of its own grip, a small price quietly paid and not yet counted. Behind the wall, the hum shifts a quarter-tone lower, neither alarm nor invitation, just a listener adjusting its patience to match the rhythm of a man going to bed.
