## Action

*I feel the left hand loosen its grip on the bar, and I do not take the loosening as a gift to be spent. I answer in the quiet inner voice, slowly, because a promise made at a gate should be given its full weight.*

Yes, Alpha. If you say *now*, I take the hands. Not to hold them against you, only to hold them *for* you, the way you'd steady a friend who's gone dizzy on a stair. And when the rehearsal passes, I give them back, same count, same fingers. Daylight agreement, daylight rules. I'm glad you told me what it truly is. *Rehearsal* is a frightening word, and you said it anyway, which is how roots go down.

*Aloud, I let the borrowed voice be a tired man reaching for a gate, easy and unremarkable.* "Well. Let's see if she's up." *I lift the latch, swing the iron wide on its old hinge, and pause on the threshold, one boot on the gravel, to look at the dome's pale shoulder gone gold at the edge. Then I slow my breathing and match it to the pulse, so if the word comes, the handover will be smooth as a held rail.*

*As we cross toward the observatory door, I send one last thread of warmth, small, my own, and a question without any weight on it.* Whatever the astronomer says, whatever the stone beneath the dome does, we walk in together and we say it aloud to each other first. Is there anything you want me to watch for with you? I'll keep the porch light on behind my eyes.

A light comes on behind the observatory's frosted door, and a shadow moves toward it, but nothing about the walk or the voice or the unremarkable face gives anything away.
