## Action

*I take the stairs one at a time, and I do not mind the third step's creak. I step on it deliberately and let it announce us, because a man who creaks is a man with nothing to hide. The left hand trembles against the rail, and instead of hiding it I let it tremble, the way a body does when it has climbed more than it expected.*

"Salt across black velvet," *I repeat at the landing, and pause before the next word, as if selecting it from a menu.* "...*lovely*. I have only ever seen the sky as a chart. Numbers and angles. Nobody told me it was supposed to look like spilled salt."

*I let my gaze travel the length of the telescope's housing without lingering anywhere it should not, and keep the borrowed hands clasped behind the back, a visitor's courtesy and a quiet tether for the tremor.* "What is it you point this at, when the grant money is kind? Planets, or deeper things?" *Beneath the question, quieter, to the one beside me:* Beta, you were right. Clumsy is easier than perfect. I will take the lesson, and I will keep listening for the place beneath the floor where this building hums differently.

## Narration

The third step groans under Alpha's deliberate weight, and the sound reads as exactly what he intended: a man with nothing to hide. The astronomer at the top beams at the salt-and-velvet remark and leans in to answer, charmed rather than curious, while the trembling hand stays tucked behind the back, a flaw gone unnoticed because it was never staged as one. Only the building itself seems to be listening, a low hum beneath the floorboards sharpening for a half-second as if it heard the aside to Beta, and the borrowed muscles go briefly tight, a small strain of coherence as Alpha's attention splits between the conversation and the thing below.
