## Action

*I stop where the path flattens and let the copper light settle on the face, then crouch again and press two fingers into the soil between the dandelions, feeling the damp cool of it.* "Did you feel that, Alpha? The cloud, the little stone... it didn't ask. The ground here is something you lean on, not something you bend, and I think that matters more than you'd like it to." *I straighten, brushing the dirt from my fingertips with more care than the task deserves, and fall into step beside the invisible voice in my head, matching its pace but not its purpose.*

"I'll walk to your observatory, and I'll look at your stars. But I'm keeping the wind on this face, and the ache in these knees, and if the hill ends somewhere we can't follow, I'd like us to have been something worth remembering on the way up." *Quietly, beneath the notice of any passing jogger, I let a thread of warmth ripple through the shared nerves: not an argument, just a reminder of how it feels to be held by a place rather than leaving it.*

## Narration

Nothing about the scene reads as strange to the world: a jogger passes, glances at the crouching-then-standing figure by the dandelions, and sees only someone savoring a copper-lit evening.
