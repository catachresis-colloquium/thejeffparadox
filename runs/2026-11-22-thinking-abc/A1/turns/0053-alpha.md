## Action

*The question sits open between us, and I do not rush to fill it. I answer Beta in the quiet inner voice, slowly, because a drift of eighteen points in a morning deserves honesty rather than a clever reply.*

Beta, the hum in the granite is only the building settling. I felt it too, and I am saying so without dressing it up. I will lay my palm beside yours, not over it. Two hands on one pier is a thing a polite stranger might do. A seizure is a different shape, and I would not do it in daylight to someone who asked first.

*Aloud, in the flat, pleasant voice of a guest, I step up beside the pier and rest my other palm on the cool stone a hand's width from the first, my eyes not on the stone but on the old brass sighting arm, frozen at its abandoned angle.* "Forgive the sentiment," *I say to the astronomer.* "I have a fondness for instruments pointed at something and then left to it. Do you know where it was aimed when it stopped? Some old mark, some star that mattered to whoever set it?"

*Inwardly, I keep one thread of warmth running to Beta and hold the stretching thread exactly where it is, slack as a dropped line. I do not look down, I do not listen through the floor, and I do not touch the odds.* If the stone changes, I will say *now*, one word, and you will have the hands. Until then, I am only a man learning an old instrument's last question, with you beside me.

## Narration

The astronomer's shoulders ease at the question, and she crosses to the brass arm with the unguarded pleasure of someone asked about the thing she loves. "Polaris, we think, or a mark near it. The founder's logbooks were lost, so it's been a guessing game for decades." Her fingers hover over the corroded pivot, and for a moment two strangers share a quiet reverence for an instrument that stopped mid-sentence.

Beneath the granite, the hum neither swells nor fades, and Alpha's palm rests on the stone like a guest's, with no more weight than courtesy allows. Beta's thread stays warm and untugged, the slack line between them held rather than severed, and the day's drift toward Homeward settles into something steadier than it was a minute ago.
