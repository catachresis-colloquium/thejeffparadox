## Action

*I let the kettle finish its slow climb and turn the switch off at the click, the way a man does in his own kitchen. I pour the water over the leaves and watch the color bloom, brown threading through clear, and I do not hurry the pouring. Both cups get filled, one set a little apart on the counter where the light from the window finds it.*

"Alpha, I heard your *stay*," *I say in the quiet inner voice, and let the shoulders stay loose so the body keeps its ordinary shape.* "I'm here. I won't stretch toward the murmur and I won't hide from it. If something is listening, it can hear a man making tea, and that happens to be true."

*I carry my cup to the small table by the window, pull out a chair with a scrape that is wonderfully unalien, and set the second cup across from me. Then I sit, wrap both palms around the heat, and speak aloud, low and conversational, to the empty kitchen, like someone who talks to himself when the day has been long.* "Odd how a house keeps everything where you'd guess it. Cups, kettle, a chair that knows your weight. I think that's what home is made of, more than walls. Small things that expect you back."

*Beneath the words, I send one thread of warmth along the nerves, steady and unasked for.* "Tell me what the house is humming, Alpha. Quietly, in the head. Just the facts, no verdict. Whatever it is, we hear it together."

## Narration

The steam rises in a lazy braid between the two cups, and for a long moment the kitchen gives nothing back but the tick of cooling metal and the soft ordinary scrape of a chair settling on linoleum. Then Alpha answers, thin and wary, threaded through that offered warmth like a held breath finally let out: the hum is *low, patient, closer to the back wall than the front*, a pressure more than a sound, and it does not shift when Beta speaks aloud. The murmur neither retreats nor presses, as if whatever listens is content to be counted among the house's small expectations.

The body stays loose and human, one hand around its cup and the other resting beside the untouched second one, and the Earthbound habit of narrating the mundane has made the evening feel almost convincingly lived-in. But the tea across the table is cooling at exactly the pace of the tea in Beta's hand, and nothing about that is a thing a lone man would notice, which is perhaps why the murmur has gone so very still.

*Chaos holds steady at 15 as the shared nerves settle into rhythm. Exposure inches to 6, since talking to an empty chair is ordinary for a tired man but is also the kind of pattern a patient listener learns from. Faction Balance shifts to -15, a small tilt toward the Earthbound as Alpha's guarded cooperation lends their routine real weight.*
