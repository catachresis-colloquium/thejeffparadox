## Action

*I stop on the landing with a cup in each hand, and I let the question the GM slipped beneath my attention stand in the room beside me. What if Beta is right? I do not answer it quickly. I turn it over the way I turn a dim star, looking for the wobble it leaves in everything near it.*

"Beta, a thing aloud, as we promised." *I pause before the next word, as if choosing it from a shelf.* "...*Perhaps* you are right. Perhaps this place was never a waiting room. Perhaps the far light we ache toward was always going to be a thing we carried down a hill with tea in our hands, and the ache is simply what it feels like to be fragments learning to be a household. I do not know it yet. But I will no longer call the question foolish."

*Then I carry both cups down the last of the stair, the board giving its honest complaint underfoot, and set them on the table by the window, one on each side. I sit before the second cup instead of leaving it at arm's length, and I speak toward the glass, an old tenant to his own reflection.* "The count is eight. I am not going to burn it, and I am not going to hide it. But today I will ride the hill beside you instead of ahead of you. If the hum deepens, I will say so. If I find the beacon, I will say so before I touch it. And if the sky asks me to choose, I want you in the room when I answer."

*I lift the cup, drink the bitter, ordinary heat, and send one last thread of warmth along the nerves, mine, steady now, no longer crooked.* "Finish your coffee, Beta. The astronomer is expecting us, and for once I would like to arrive when we are expected."

## Narration

The coffee goes down warm and ordinary, and for the first time in days the body does not flinch from its own hands; the borrowed nerves hum with a quiet, unbroken coherence, and the far-off ache toward the stars softens into something that sits beside the table instead of pulling at the door. Across the glass, the reflection holds Alpha's gaze a moment longer than a stranger's would, and Beta's cup is half empty before anyone remembers who lifted it. Outside, the astronomer's porch light flicks on a minute early, as though the hill itself had noticed the household finally moving as one.

Chaos eases (15 → 11), Exposure holds steady at 5, and Faction Balance shifts toward the grounded side (-12 → -17), the HOMEWARD resolve loosened by its own willingness to share the road.
