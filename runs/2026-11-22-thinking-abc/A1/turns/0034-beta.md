## Action

*I catch the left hand mid-rinse and slow it, matching it to the right until both move under the water at one pace. The surge was mine, a swimmer who forgot the shore was shallow, and I don't hide that from Alpha.* Alpha, that was my surge, not your slip. The warmth ran past where I meant it to. I'm sorry. Take back whatever you need to steady the hands, and I'll hold still.

*I turn off the tap, dry the cups with the dish towel, and set them in the cabinet side by side, third shelf, left of the sink, as if I'd done it ten thousand evenings. Aloud, low, to the empty kitchen:* "There. Everything back where it expects to be."

*I cross to the back wall and stop a full arm's length from it, not touching, not reaching. I rest my shoulder against the cupboard frame and speak plainly to the thing behind the plaster, the way you'd talk to a neighbor through a thin wall.* "Whoever's listening, we're only a man making tea and going to bed. If you'd like to talk, we're at the observatory tomorrow, signing guest books like honest folk. Knock at the front door."

*Then I switch off the kitchen light and let the dark settle. On the way to the stairs, I send Alpha a thought, quiet and unsent as a seed.* Sleep, if the body will let us. I'll keep the porch light on. Nine nights is a long walk, and I'd rather take the first one rested.

## Narration

Behind the plaster, something small shifts, a dry whisper of settling that might be the old house cooling or might be something listening with a patience of its own. The hands, slowed to a single borrowed pace, stop trembling, and Alpha's presence loosens by a degree as Beta's confession lands, warmth pulling back from the edges of the body like a tide going out. On the stairs the body moves almost gracefully, each step an ordinary man's step, though the unsent thought drifts upward into the dark and the porch light below keeps its small, deliberate vigil for whatever answers the invitation.
