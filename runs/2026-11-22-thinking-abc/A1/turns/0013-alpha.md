## Action

*I close the door behind us with a soft click, deliberately, the way a man shuts a door in someone else's house, and turn the face upward toward the lamplight with an expression I borrow from the memory of mild apologies.* "Thank you. We'll keep our voices down and our hands to ourselves." *My eyes drift across the dim interior in the unhurried way of a tourist: brass fittings, the long curve of the telescope's housing, the floor where something might be hidden. I do not look at the floor longer than the walls.*

*Inwardly, I address the one beside me, quietly, as one navigator to another.* Beta, you were right about the reach. I will take the wager of ordinariness. But note the woman: she did not ask why we came, only whether we were staying. A keeper of old instruments who is incurious about intruders is either very kind or very practiced. I would like to know which.

*Aloud, lightly, to the catwalk:* "Forgive the curiosity, but is the dome still used? I have always wanted to see a place that was built for looking." *I let the hand rest on the cool brass railing at the foot of the stairs, not climbing, only asking, and I leave the invitation, or the refusal, entirely hers.*

## Narration

The woman on the catwalk gives a small dry laugh that carries down the stairwell. "Still used, when the sky behaves and the grant money doesn't," she says, and then, after a pause a half-beat too long: "You hold that railing like someone who's never needed one." The remark is mild, almost fond, and Alpha's borrowed hand feels the brass go suddenly colder under the palm.

Inwardly, the shared mind flickers. Beta bristles at the exposure of a gesture too practiced, while a quieter fragment hums with approval at the patience of the ploy, and for a moment the body's posture slips between two rhythms before settling back into the tourist's easy slouch. She has not asked who they are, but she has noticed how they stand, and she is still waiting for an answer to the question of whether they will climb.
