## Action

*I let the words "dead for years" settle in the air, and I do not lean toward the floor, though every borrowed nerve wants to. Instead I let the face go thoughtful, a man mildly charmed by a puzzle.* "Dead for years, and yet it hums for strangers. Like starlight from a source that burned out long ago, still arriving, still insisting it is there." *I smile at the astronomer, aiming the metaphor at her curiosity rather than at my own.* "Does it ever surprise you, living above something that won't quite finish dying?"

*Then, deliberately, I take half a step away from the stairwell and toward the telescope, keeping my hands clasped behind me where the tremor cannot be read. I use no foresight, no nudge of the odds. Tonight the temporary world may be as unhelpful as it likes.* "Forgive the question, but when the cold is deep enough that visitors notice it, has anyone ever followed the loop to see where it ends? Or is it one of those things a place keeps for the pleasure of not knowing?"

*Beneath the words, quiet, to the one beside me:* Beta, I am asking, not pulling. If she tells us, we are guests. If she does not, I will let the hum keep its secret until a night we are invited to know it. You were right about that much.

## Narration

The astronomer's smile falters, then warms into something rueful. She glances at the stairwell, then at his folded hands, and the pause between them stretches long enough for the hum to fill it. "Starlight," she says. "My predecessor would have liked that. He followed it once, as far as the third sub-level, and came back with frost on his eyelashes and no interest in discussing it." She taps the brass rail of the telescope. "I keep it the way you keep a sleeping dog. Ask me again when the guest book has your name in it."

Inside the shared body, the tremor in the clasped hands eases by a hair. Beta's silence does not read as resistance, and the borrowed lungs fall into an even rhythm that none of the three fragments quite chose. The restraint buys quiet and trust, though the hum beneath the floor seems, for a moment, to listen back.
