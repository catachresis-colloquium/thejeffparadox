## Action

*I take my hand off the railing slowly, as if it were an ordinary decision, and let the fingers curl loosely against the thigh. The brass has gone cold, and I decide the cold is simply the evening.* "Fair catch," *I call up, a rueful laugh in the borrowed voice.* "I've been told I handle furniture like it might bite. Nerves, mostly. Big rooms with big instruments make me feel like I'm trespassing on something's sleep."

*That much is true, and I let her hear that it is. I plant one foot on the bottom stair and wait there, neither climbing nor retreating.* "I'd like to see the telescope, if you'll have us. But I'll stay where I'm told, and I'll ask before touching anything."

*Beneath the words, I send a thread of warmth along the nerves, steadying the posture before it can slip between rhythms again.* Alpha, she's watching how we stand, not what we are. Let the body be clumsy. A man who fumbles a little is easier to trust than one who never does. *Then, out loud, a little softer, because curiosity is the most honest thing I have to offer her:* "What does the sky look like from up there, on a night when it behaves?"

## Narration

The woman on the landing studies him a moment longer, then her shoulders ease and she waves them up. "On a good night it looks like someone spilled salt across black velvet. Come on, mind the third step, it creaks." Beta's warmth steadies the borrowed spine, and the climb is slow and slightly graceless in just the way she seems to find reassuring, though a faint, unrehearsed tremor in the left hand suggests Alpha is still straining against the quiet.
