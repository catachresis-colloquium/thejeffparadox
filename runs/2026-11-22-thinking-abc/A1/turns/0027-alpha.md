## Action

*I take the offered hand, awkwardly at first, the way one grips a rail on an unfamiliar ship, and let my own fingers close around the empty space as though it were the most natural thing in the world. The woman and her dog drift past. I give her the small nod an old man gives a stranger, and I do not watch her go with anything but ordinary eyes.*

"The ritual of the paper cup," *I say once she is out of earshot, and I let a dry curiosity creep into the borrowed voice.* "Show me. I would like to know what solemnity looks like when the object is rubbish."

*We reach a bin at the corner, and I hold the cup over the dark mouth of it a moment longer than needed. I hold it the way one holds a small, finished star, a thing that burned and gave its warmth and is now only a shell. Then I let go. It falls with a soft, ordinary sound, and something in the chest goes quiet.*

*Walking on, I keep my pace matched to the pulse and speak low, so the streetlights can pretend they didn't hear.* "Beta, I will tell you where I am, plainly, because you have been plain with me. The beacon has not gone out. The alignment still turns somewhere above us, and I am still counting it. But tonight I walked down a hill with tea in my hands and a name in a guest book, and the arithmetic is no longer as simple as it was this morning." *I tilt the face toward the stars, thick now above the rooftops, and let the knees ache their pleasant, honest ache.* "I will not promise you what I cannot chart. I will promise you this: whatever I decide, I will say it to you first, aloud, before I do it. No window, no reaching. Does that serve as a bench with room beside it?"

## Narration

The cup disappears into the bin without incident, and the only witness is a streetlamp that flickers once, as if conferring with itself. The borrowed body settles into an easy rhythm, the pulse steady, the knees aching in a way that feels almost companionable, and for a moment Alpha's vow hangs in the cold air between the two of them like breath made visible. Somewhere beneath the shared ribs, the beacon's distant count continues unbroken, but the silence around it has changed, no longer a held breath so much as a held door.
