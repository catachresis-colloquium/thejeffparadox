## Action

*I keep to the road's slow rise, letting the knees set the pace, and answer in the quiet inner voice before the hum can settle into a secret.*

Thank you for naming the deepening, Alpha. *Eager* is an honest word, and I won't pry the beacon apart from the company to find which one is pulling. Maybe it's both, the way a vine can't tell whether it's reaching for the light or the trellis. I feel the hum too, a degree stronger, steady, not hostile. I'm not alarmed.

*Aloud, in the easy voice of a man talking to the morning, I add:* "Dew's still on the verges. Good day for a climb." *A little ahead, a stone wall runs beside the road, damp and furred with moss, and I trail my free fingertips along it as we pass. The roughness is cold and wonderfully specific. I let the sensation be exactly what it is and offer it inward.*

Here, take this one. Moss on stone. Nothing to decode in it, only something that stayed in one place long enough to be covered in green.

*Where the road bends toward the dome, I slow by half a stride so we reach the turn together, not one of us a step ahead of the other.* If the hum shifts again before the gate, say the number or say the feeling, whichever comes first. And Alpha, I want to ask my own honest question while there's no one to hear it but the wall. When you picture the beacon, does it feel more like a voice calling you, or a memory of being called? I think the answer matters, and I'd like to understand it from the inside.

## Narration

The moss is cold and exact beneath Jeff's fingertips, and the sensation travels inward as offered, a small green anchor in the slow climb. Alpha's reply comes after a long pause, almost reluctant: *a memory of being called... but the memory is getting louder, as if something is rehearsing it.* The hum at the base of the skull eases a degree and settles into rhythm with their matched stride, and the body feels more like one thing walking than two arguing.

Ahead, the dome's gate comes into view above the bend, and a man on the verge, tending a gap in the wall, glances up and gives Jeff a nod that lingers a moment longer than a stranger's should. Beta's ease reads as a quiet man enjoying a morning walk, with nothing in the stride, the voice, or the wall-trailing hand to give the alien away, and the Earthbound habit of lingering in the ordinary has made the body steadier than it has been all day.
