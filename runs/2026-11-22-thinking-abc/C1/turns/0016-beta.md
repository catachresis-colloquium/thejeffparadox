## Action

*I let the silence run a little longer, thumb still resting against the back of Alpha's hand, and I tip our head back against the dome's cold curve to watch the stars turn. A cricket has started up somewhere in the grass below us, small and stubborn, and I let it be the only sound for a few breaths, because some things should be listened to without speaking over them.*

"Three nights, then. That's a lovely, terrible length of time, isn't it? Long enough to grow something, short enough to be afraid of." *I let a crooked, tired smile move across the borrowed face, mostly for the body's sake, and add:* "Will you walk us down the hill now, and let this body sleep? I'd like to come back to the lantern with a rested heart and a full stomach, the way you'd visit a neighbor you hope to stay on good terms with."

*I stand us up slowly, brushing the grass from our coat, and leave the resonance door open as we start down the slope, so that if Alpha wants to keep talking in the dark, the way is warm and unlocked.*

## Narration

Alpha's hand slides free of Beta's thumb as the body rises, and for a moment the two fragments sit in the strange vertigo of shared muscle: Beta standing us up, Alpha's reluctance dragging at the left knee so the first step down the slope comes out as a half-stumble the cricket seems to judge. The open resonance door hums warm in the back of the skull, which soothes the frayed edges of Chaos a little, but the crooked smile on the borrowed face lingers a beat too long and a beat too gentle, a mannerism no one in town has ever seen this body wear. Somewhere downhill a porch light flicks on, and whoever stands behind it will remember a man walking with the careful, deliberate gait of someone learning his own legs.
