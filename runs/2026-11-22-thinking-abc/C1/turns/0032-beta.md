## Action

*I let the borrowed eyelids stay closed and take a long, slow breath, giving Alpha's confession the same quiet it deserves, like a seed pressed into cold ground before anyone knows whether it will take. Then I answer inward, soft as a hand on a shoulder.* "Thank you for telling me while I was holding the watch, Alpha. That kind of honesty is the opposite of a bell in the night, and I felt how much it cost you to set it down."

*I keep the resonance door dimmed and the lantern untouched, and I offer the next part as an invitation, not a bargain.* "I won't ask you to unsay what you came here for. Carrying a message home is a real thing, and maybe it's even a kind thing. But I'd ask that we write it together, in daylight, with the body fed and rested. The message could say we're alive and well and still learning what home is, instead of saying only that we're ready to leave."

*Then I turn a little toward the unnamed pulse behind our ribs, still humming that wordless tune.* "And whoever you are, you heard that too. You're allowed an opinion about the letter."

## Narration

The inner quiet holds for three heartbeats, then Alpha's reply comes as a faint pressure behind the sternum, grudging but unmistakably a yes to the shared draft. Beneath it, the unnamed pulse stops humming mid-phrase and answers with a single shaky note, the first sound it has made that wasn't borrowed. The body's hands twitch against the blanket, trying to follow three conversations at once, and the room tilts a hair as coherence frays at the seams, though no one outside the skull has noticed.

Exposure holds at 9, since nothing has leaked past the eyelids. Chaos stays pinned at 100 while the fragments strain toward a fragile consensus. Faction Balance shifts toward the Earthbound, to -7, as Alpha concedes that the message might be written in daylight.
